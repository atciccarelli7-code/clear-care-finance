## 4. Evidence and factual boundaries

Primary/authoritative sources used:

1. CMS, Acute Inpatient Prospective Payment System — Medicare acute inpatient prospective payment and MS-DRG framework.
   - https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/prospective-payment-systems/acute-inpatient-pps
2. CMS, FY 2026 IPPS Final Rule Home Page — current MS-DRG weights, geometric/arithmetic mean length-of-stay tables, and FY 2026 payment files.
   - https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/prospective-payment-systems/acute-inpatient-pps/fy-2026-ipps-final-rule-home-page
3. CMS, Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program — excess-readmission payment reductions, capped at 3 percent.
   - https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/prospective-payment-systems/acute-inpatient-pps/hospital-readmissions-reduction-program-hrrp
4. CMS, Hospital-Acquired Condition Reduction Program — 1 percent payment reduction for hospitals in the worst-performing quartile on selected measures.
   - https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/prospective-payment-systems/acute-inpatient-pps/hospital-acquired-condition-reduction-program-hacrp
5. CMS, Hospital Value-Based Purchasing — 2 percent base operating MS-DRG withhold and quality-based redistribution.
   - https://www.cms.gov/medicare/quality/value-based-programs/hospital-purchasing
6. CMS, Hospital Price Transparency — gross, discounted cash, payer-specific negotiated, and de-identified negotiated price fields.
   - https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/hospital-price-transparency/hospitals
7. CMS, Hospital Outpatient Prospective Payment System — outpatient hospital payment and APC structure.
   - https://www.cms.gov/cms-guide-medical-technology-companies-and-other-interested-parties/payment/opps
8. MACPAC, Medicaid Base and Supplemental Payments to Hospitals — state variation in base, supplemental, and managed-care-directed hospital payments.
   - https://www.macpac.gov/publication/medicaid-base-and-supplemental-payments-to-hospitals/

Factual guardrails deliberately preserved:

- The article does not say every hospital or payer uses DRGs.
- The article anchors DRG discussion to many Original Medicare acute inpatient stays.
- The four-day/day-five example is explicitly illustrative, not a claimed break-even threshold.
- Fixed and semi-fixed hospital costs are acknowledged; an extra day is described as consuming resources and capacity rather than creating an entirely new day of cost from zero.
- Medicare outlier payments and other adjustments are acknowledged.
- Readmission, hospital-acquired-condition, and value-based purchasing incentives are included so the article does not imply that Medicare simply rewards premature discharge.
- Commercial and Medicaid payment are described as varying rather than copied from Medicare.

## 6. Editorial differentiation and redundancy review

PASS.

The article overlaps intentionally with existing CAF pieces but has a distinct thesis:

- `$20 Tylenol` explains charge versus payment.
- `Why Hospitals Care So Much About Length of Stay` explains the LOS/capacity metric.
- `Shock Absorber` explains why hospitals inherit downstream failures.
- `How a Hospital Actually Makes Money` connects case-based payment, payer variation, hospital cost, documentation, length of stay, quality incentives, and the tension between business goals and patient care.

The overlap creates a coherent hospital-economics cluster rather than duplicating the same article.
